# Proposed Educational Learning and Performance Management System

## A Multi-School, Interactive LMS with Teacher-Assisted Artificial Intelligence

## Abstract

This proposed system is a multi-school Learning Management System (LMS) designed to provide schools with a centralized yet privacy-preserving environment for managing teaching, learning, assessment, student performance, and academic engagement. The system is conceptually similar to an LMS such as Google Classroom but is designed specifically around the organizational structures and educational requirements of individual schools. Each participating school maintains its own users, subjects, classes, learning spaces, academic records, permissions, and performance information while operating within a shared platform.

A major component of the proposed system is its integration of gamification and artificial intelligence (AI). Gamification is intended to encourage student participation and engagement, while AI functions primarily as an assistant for teachers by helping generate ideas for quizzes, assignments, activities, questions, learning materials, and other educational resources. AI does not independently control the instructional workflow; teachers remain responsible for reviewing, modifying, approving, and publishing AI-assisted content.

The system also incorporates a hierarchical authorization and membership model in which users may self-register but require appropriate approval before gaining access to schools, administrative functions, or specific learning environments. Through the combination of school-level privacy, role-based authorization, interactive learning, performance monitoring, gamification, and teacher-controlled AI assistance, the proposed system aims to provide a unified educational environment that supports both instructional management and continuous student development.

## 1. Introduction

The increasing use of digital technologies in education has created opportunities for schools to improve the way learning materials, assessments, communication, and student performance are managed. Traditional learning management systems commonly provide mechanisms for teachers to distribute materials, create assignments, administer assessments, and communicate with students. However, a general-purpose LMS may not always reflect the organizational structure, privacy requirements, academic workflows, and performance-monitoring needs of individual schools.

The proposed system addresses this concept by developing a multi-school educational platform in which different schools can operate within the same system while maintaining independent academic environments. Each school can have its own administrators, teachers, students, subjects, classes, learning rooms, and academic activities. Access to information is controlled according to the user's role and relationship with the school or learning environment.

The system is further designed around the idea that learning should not be limited to the distribution and collection of assignments. Student participation, assessment results, learning activities, progress, and achievements can collectively contribute to a broader representation of student performance. Gamification can provide additional mechanisms for encouraging participation, while artificial intelligence can assist teachers in preparing educational materials and activities.

The central principle of the proposed system is therefore not to replace existing educational roles with automation, but to provide technology that **supports teachers, engages students, and gives schools better visibility into the learning process**.

## 2. General Concept of the System

The proposed platform can be viewed as a **multi-school educational ecosystem** composed of interconnected learning and management environments. Although schools operate on the same platform, each school functions as an independent organizational boundary. A school administrator manages the school's environment, teachers manage their assigned instructional spaces, and students interact with the learning environments to which they have been authorized.

The system follows a continuous educational relationship:

**School → Teacher → Learning Environment → Student → Learning Activity → Assessment → Performance → Feedback → Improved Learning**

The platform records relevant learning activities throughout this process, allowing performance information to become more meaningful than an isolated collection of grades. Teachers can observe how students participate, complete activities, perform in assessments, and progress over time.

Artificial intelligence operates alongside this workflow as a support mechanism. Instead of allowing AI to independently determine what students should learn or automatically publish educational content, the system places the teacher at the center of the decision-making process. AI-generated content is treated as a suggestion that requires human review.

## 3. Multi-School Environment

A fundamental characteristic of the system is its ability to support multiple schools within one platform. Each school represents an independent organizational environment with its own users, academic structures, learning activities, and performance information.

The system must therefore distinguish between **platform-level administration** and **school-level administration**. The platform administrator manages the overall ecosystem, while the school administrator manages the school's internal environment. A school administrator should not automatically gain access to another school's information simply because both schools operate on the same platform.

This separation establishes a privacy boundary between schools and allows the platform to support different educational institutions without merging their academic data.

The model can therefore be represented conceptually as:

**Platform**

→ School A

→ School B

→ School C

→ School N

Each school independently contains its own:

**Users → Subjects → Classes → Learning Rooms → Activities → Assessments → Performance**

## 4. User Registration and Authorization

The proposed system uses a self-registration concept in which users can create their own accounts rather than requiring every account to be manually created by an administrator. However, self-registration does not automatically grant access to a school or privileged functionality.

The distinction between **identity and authorization** is fundamental to the system.

A person may successfully create an account, but the account must still establish an approved relationship with a school or learning environment before accessing protected educational resources.

For example, an individual intending to become a School Administrator may register and submit a request to establish or manage a school. The request is reviewed by the appropriate platform authority. A teacher may register independently and request to join a particular school, but the teacher becomes an authorized member of that school only after the School Administrator accepts the request. Similarly, students may register independently but must be authorized to access the appropriate school and learning environment.

This creates a controlled hierarchy:

**Self-Registration → Pending Request → Appropriate Authority → Approval → Membership → Authorized Access**

This model preserves the convenience of self-registration while preventing unauthorized individuals from simply assigning themselves privileged roles.

## 5. Role and Responsibility Structure

The system is organized around several major roles.

### 5.1 Super Administrator

The Super Administrator operates at the platform level and is responsible for maintaining the overall educational ecosystem. This role manages the relationship between the platform and participating schools, including approval of appropriate school-level administrative requests. The Super Administrator establishes the highest level of trust within the platform but does not need to participate in ordinary classroom activities.

### 5.2 School Administrator

The School Administrator manages a specific school's environment. After receiving appropriate authorization, the School Administrator manages school users, academic structures, teachers, students, and school-level configurations. The administrator controls which teachers and students become members of the school while remaining restricted from other schools' environments.

### 5.3 Teacher

The Teacher is the primary instructional role. Teachers manage their authorized learning environments, prepare learning resources, create assignments and quizzes, evaluate student submissions, provide feedback, and monitor student performance. Teachers also represent the primary human authority over AI-generated educational content.

### 5.4 Student

The Student is the primary learner and participant within the system. Students access authorized learning environments, consume learning resources, participate in activities, complete assignments and assessments, receive feedback, and interact with gamification features. Their activities generate information that contributes to their learning and performance records.

### 5.5 Parent or Guardian

A Parent or Guardian may provide an additional perspective on student development. Their access is limited to authorized information concerning the student they are responsible for. They may observe relevant progress, completion, achievements, and academic information without receiving administrative or instructional control.

## 6. School Membership and Learning Environment Membership

The proposed system distinguishes between **belonging to a school** and **belonging to a particular learning environment**.

A teacher may be an authorized member of a school but may only have instructional access to particular subjects or classes assigned to them. Similarly, a student may belong to a school but should not automatically have access to every classroom within that school.

A learning environment, referred to conceptually as a class or room, provides the space where teachers and students interact around a particular subject or educational activity.

The relationship can therefore be understood as:

**User → School → Subject/Class → Learning Room → Learning Activities**

This structure allows the system to maintain more precise authorization and prevents excessive access to unrelated academic information.

## 7. Teaching and Learning Workflow

The central workflow begins when a teacher establishes a learning environment for an authorized subject or class. The teacher provides learning materials, lessons, announcements, assignments, quizzes, activities, and assessments according to the needs of the students.

Students access the learning environment and participate in the activities provided by the teacher. Their submissions, assessment results, completion records, participation, and other relevant learning interactions become part of their academic activity.

The teacher subsequently evaluates the student's work and provides feedback. This feedback can influence future learning activities, creating a continuous instructional cycle rather than treating each assignment as an independent transaction.

The overall educational cycle can be expressed as:

**Prepare → Teach → Engage → Assess → Evaluate → Feedback → Improve → Teach Again**

## 8. Artificial Intelligence as a Teacher Assistant

Artificial intelligence represents one of the defining components of the proposed system. However, the intended role of AI is explicitly **assistive rather than authoritative**.

Teachers may use AI to generate possible quiz questions, assignment concepts, activity ideas, question variations, explanations, rubrics, instructional materials, or other educational suggestions. AI may also assist teachers in examining learning information and identifying possible areas that deserve attention.

However, AI-generated content is not automatically considered final educational content. The teacher remains responsible for reviewing the generated material, determining whether it is appropriate, correcting inaccuracies, adapting it to the class, and deciding whether it should be provided to students.

The intended relationship is therefore:

**Teacher → AI Assistance → Teacher Review → Teacher Decision → Student**

rather than:

**AI → Student**

This preserves human oversight and recognizes that educational decisions require contextual understanding that automated systems may not possess.

## 9. Student Performance Management

Student performance is represented through multiple forms of learning evidence rather than relying exclusively on examination scores. The system can conceptually collect information from assignments, quizzes, assessments, participation, completion, learning activities, and progress over time.

This allows teachers and authorized school personnel to observe patterns in student development.

For example, a student's performance information could help reveal whether the student is consistently completing activities, struggling with particular areas, improving over time, or requiring additional instructional attention.

The objective is not simply to produce more data, but to transform learning activity into **meaningful educational insight** that teachers can use when making instructional decisions.

## 10. Gamification and Interactive Learning

Gamification is incorporated to increase student engagement and encourage participation. Instead of treating the LMS as a purely administrative environment where students only receive assignments and submit files, the platform can provide interactive mechanisms such as achievements, points, progress indicators, challenges, milestones, or other recognition systems.

Gamification should remain subordinate to educational objectives. Its purpose is to encourage productive learning behavior rather than turn academic performance into an uncontrolled competition.

The system can therefore distinguish between:

**Learning objectives** — what students need to learn.

**Performance measurement** — how students are progressing.

**Gamification** — how the system encourages engagement with the learning process.

This separation prevents the gamification layer from replacing the actual educational purpose of the platform.

## 11. Privacy and Authorization

Because the proposed system supports multiple schools, privacy and authorization are fundamental characteristics rather than optional features.

A user's permissions should depend not only on their global role but also on their relationship with a particular school, subject, class, or learning environment. A teacher authorized in School A should not automatically gain access to School B. A student enrolled in one classroom should not automatically see another classroom's private resources.

The system therefore follows the principle of **least necessary access**, where users receive access according to their legitimate educational responsibilities.

This model also allows the system to maintain separation between administrative information, teacher information, student information, academic records, and learning activities.

## 12. Technological Concept

The proposed system is conceptually based on a modern Laravel ecosystem consisting of **Laravel, Livewire 4, Flux UI 2 Free, MySQL, and Spatie Laravel Permission**. Laravel provides the application foundation and server-side framework, while Livewire provides an interactive web application experience without requiring the entire system to be built as a separate frontend application.

Flux UI provides the interface component foundation, allowing the platform to maintain a consistent visual language. MySQL serves as the primary relational data store, while Spatie Laravel Permission supports the role and permission model required by the platform's authorization structure.

The system follows a **monolithic architectural approach**, meaning the major capabilities exist within one application while maintaining conceptual separation between domains such as identity, school management, academic management, learning environments, assessments, performance, gamification, and AI assistance.

The visual identity is centered around **teal as the primary color** and **Roboto as the primary typeface**, providing a consistent foundation for the platform's educational and technological identity.

## 14. Conceptual Goal

The ultimate goal of the proposed system is to create more than a conventional LMS. It is intended to become a **school-centered educational ecosystem** where administration, teaching, learning, assessment, student performance, engagement, and artificial intelligence exist within a unified environment.

The platform gives schools control over their own educational communities while providing teachers with tools to reduce repetitive preparation work and understand student performance more effectively. Students receive an interactive learning environment that combines academic activities with meaningful engagement mechanisms. AI provides additional assistance without removing the teacher's authority.

The core philosophy of the system can therefore be summarized as:

**A shared educational platform where schools maintain their own identity, privacy, authorization, and academic environment; teachers remain the primary instructional decision-makers; students actively participate in their learning; gamification encourages engagement; and artificial intelligence assists educators without replacing human judgment.**
